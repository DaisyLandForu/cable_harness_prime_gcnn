\stitle{Related work}.
Spacecraft wiring (cable harness) design is closely related to harness routing in automotive, aircraft, industrial panels, and other constrained 3D environments.
Across these domains, the core computational task is to connect multiple terminal pairs (or nets) through a cluttered layout while respecting geometric and engineering rules and minimizing a physical cost (e.g., length, weight, risk).
Below we organize the most relevant literature into modeling paradigms and algorithmic families, and highlight the gap addressed by this paper.

\etitle{3D routing representations and geometric post-processing.}
Most practical systems first convert a continuous 3D workspace into a discrete routing graph (regular grid, roadmap, or CAD-derived graph), where obstacles remove vertices/edges and engineering preferences are expressed as node/edge penalties (``cost fields'') \cite{karlsson2024cad}.
In parallel, there is renewed interest in improving the efficiency and geometric quality of the underlying path-search primitives.
Kim \etal{} proposed \emph{JPS--Theta*} for 3D cable routing, combining Jump Point Search and Theta* to accelerate search while allowing any-angle paths, and further applied B-spline optimization to produce natural, collision-free cable shapes \cite{kim2024jpstheta}.
These works motivate our graph-based abstraction and also suggest a two-stage pipeline (topology first, geometry smoothing later) that is compatible with spacecraft harness design.

\etitle{Harness topology optimization: multi-branch structures and bundling.}
Beyond single-cable shortest paths, harness design requires reasoning about the \emph{shared trunk and branching structure}.
Karlsson \etal{} formulated an optimization model that simultaneously (i) minimizes total cable length, (ii) rewards shared edges to promote bundling, and (iii) incorporates case-specific 3D environment information; they developed deterministic algorithms based on Lagrangian relaxation and local improvements, producing multiple topology candidates on industrial 3D cases \cite{karlsson2024cad}.
Complementary heuristic approaches combine informed graph search with meta-heuristics for multi-branch harnesses.
For example, Yang \etal{} proposed an A*--ant-colony hybrid for multi-branch cable layout, explicitly modeling routing cost components and showing improved solutions over classic A* or ACO variants \cite{yang2024astaraco}.
Compared with these harness-routing studies, our spacecraft setting shares the ``multi-terminal routing + topology'' nature but differs in (i) using \emph{weight} as the primary cost driver and (ii) imposing an explicit global \emph{acyclicity} requirement (tree/forest wiring without redundant loops).

\etitle{Optimization formulations, hardness, and exact solvers.}
Many routing and interconnection-design problems can be expressed as variants of Steiner tree/forest, multi-commodity flow, or more general network-design formulations.
These formulations are computationally intractable in general: Steiner tree is NP-complete \cite{garey1979computers}, and Steiner-forest-type connectivity objectives remain NP-hard \cite{goemans1995primal}.
Karlsson \etal{} also noted that their objective reduces to Steiner tree in a special case, thereby inheriting NP-hardness \cite{karlsson2024cad}.
For exact (or provably near-optimal) solutions on moderate instances, mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) with modern solvers remains a strong baseline.
General-purpose solvers such as SCIP \cite{achterberg2009scip} and commercial optimizers (e.g., COPT \cite{copt2024}) provide branch-and-bound/branch-and-cut engines and can incorporate rich linear constraints.
To enforce acyclicity, classic modeling tools include subtour-elimination constraints and ordering-based constraints such as Miller--Tucker--Zemlin (MTZ) \cite{miller1960integer}, as well as flow-based arborescence constraints.
Our exact baseline follows this tradition, while also serving as a reference for validating heuristic solutions.

\etitle{Deterministic heuristics with bounds and multi-solution generation.}
When the graph or the number of terminal pairs grows, deterministic heuristics are often preferred for speed, controllability, and reproducibility.
An effective pattern is \emph{relax-and-repair}: relax difficult coupling constraints (e.g., via Lagrangian relaxation), solve decomposed shortest-path subproblems, and then apply local search/repair to obtain feasible topologies.
This is the central idea in \cite{karlsson2024cad}, where projected subgradient updates on dual multipliers guide the generation of diverse initial solutions, followed by routing heuristics that refine topology and approximate the Pareto front.
Our method follows the same high-level philosophy but targets a different feasibility region: we incorporate an explicit acyclicity constraint and design a deterministic procedure that can return the best and near-best (suboptimal) \emph{acyclic} routing topologies for downstream engineering selection.

\etitle{Learning-assisted routing and learning-based combinatorial optimization.}
Recent work has explored learning for 3D cable routing in CAD workflows.
Kim and Kwon investigated deep reinforcement learning for automatic cable routing in 3D electrical panels, integrating the agent into a CAD loop and reporting competitive path quality \cite{kim2025drlpanel}.
In adjacent ``cable route planning'' domains, reinforcement learning has also been used for multi-objective submarine cable routing \cite{zhao2024submarine}.
At a more abstract level, learning-based solvers for Steiner-type problems have progressed via neural-algorithmic hybrids (e.g., combining neural scoring with guided search) \cite{kahng2024nnsteiner} and instance-reduction/pruning strategies \cite{zhang2024prunesteiner}; benchmarks such as SteBen aim to standardize evaluation for neural Steiner solvers \cite{park2024steben}.
These learning-based directions are promising for scalability and warm-starting, but often provide limited optimality guarantees.
In contrast, our focus is a \emph{traditional}, fully deterministic algorithm that (i) admits an exact MILP formulation for correctness and benchmarking, and (ii) extends to a fast heuristic capable of generating multiple high-quality acyclic candidates.

\etitle{Cable-specific feasibility constraints: tangling, loops, and topology control.}
Finally, while many studies emphasize collision avoidance, cable systems also face cable-specific hazards such as snagging, tangling, and undesired loops.
For tethered mobile robots, Shimada \etal{} proposed tangle- and contact-free path planning to mitigate entanglement risks \cite{shimada2024tcfpp}.
Although our application differs, these results highlight that controlling loop-like behaviors is practically important.
Our explicit acyclicity constraint can be viewed as a structural safeguard that rules out redundant loop segments in spacecraft harness topologies.

\etitle{Positioning of this work.}
In summary, existing harness routing literature provides strong foundations in (i) graph-based modeling with environment costs, (ii) MILP formulations with solver baselines, and (iii) deterministic relax-and-repair heuristics for scalable candidate generation.
We contribute a spacecraft-oriented formulation that adds a global acyclicity constraint and a deterministic algorithmic framework that unifies: an exact solver-based baseline and a heuristic extension that returns the optimal and near-optimal acyclic routing solutions.
